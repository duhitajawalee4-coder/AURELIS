5 Ways to Make a Small Kitchen Feel More Spacious

Ideas, practical guidance and design thinking from the AURELIS Journal.

1. Keep the material palette focused

Too many competing textures can make a compact room feel busy. Choose one main surface and one supporting finish, then repeat them intentionally.

2. Use lighter visual tones

Light surfaces can help reflect available light and make boundaries feel less heavy. Warm stone, soft beige and pale grey can also create an open feeling.

3. Reduce visual breaks

Continuing a countertop material into a backsplash can visually connect two areas. Fewer abrupt changes make the room easier to read.

4. Let cabinetry stay simple

Clean-lined doors and consistent hardware keep attention on the architecture and surfaces rather than creating lots of visual interruptions.

5. Use lighting deliberately

Under-cabinet lighting can reveal texture and brighten work areas. Layered lighting also helps a small kitchen feel more dimensional in the evening.

AURELIS tip: Start with how the space is used, then choose the material that supports both function and visual character.
