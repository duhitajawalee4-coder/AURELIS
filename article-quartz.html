Quartz vs Granite: Which Is Right for a Modern Kitchen?

Ideas, practical guidance and design thinking from the AURELIS Journal.

Quartz: a controlled, clean look

Quartz is often chosen when consistency matters. Its engineered composition allows a more predictable colour and pattern, which can make it easier to coordinate with cabinetry, backsplashes and flooring.

Granite: natural variation and character

Granite brings natural mineral variation, so every slab can have its own movement and visual rhythm. That makes it useful when the countertop is intended to become a noticeable part of the room.

What should you compare?

Look at maintenance expectations, the amount of visual variation you want, the surrounding colour palette and how heavily the kitchen will be used. View a physical sample where possible, because surfaces can read differently under your home lighting.

AURELIS tip: Start with how the space is used, then choose the material that supports both function and visual character.
